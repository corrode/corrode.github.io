Your Mentoring Journey

  1. Tell Me About Your Goals

    Share your background, what you'd like to achieve, and the time you have available in the form below. I'll review your answers and, if I think I can help, invite you to a free 30-minute intro call. We'll use that time to discuss your goals and decide whether working together makes sense.

  2. Make a Plan

    We turn your goals into a plan around your project and experience. Together, we choose what to tackle first and set a regular session schedule that gives you time to apply what you learn. We agree on the scope and pricing before you commit.

  3. Work Through Real Problems

    We typically meet every two or three weeks to discuss your Rust work. Bring a pull request, architecture questions, or a piece of code you'd like feedback on. We'll discuss your questions and pair-program together. You leave with a clearer understanding and concrete next steps.

  4. Achieve Your Goals

    Whether your goal is to find a Rust job, ship an ambitious project, or become better at reviewing Rust code, you'll leave with the mental models and a process for putting your knowledge into practice. What you learn will stay with you throughout your career.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this right for my experience level?

This is for experienced software engineers who want to get better at working with Rust. You might be moving from Python, Go, C++, or another language, or already using Rust and looking for feedback on your design and code-review decisions. For organization-wide support, see my team consulting and training.

Do I need an existing Rust project?

No. Your own project gives us useful context, but we can also choose a realistic project or work through design and review exercises based on the kind of Rust work you want to do.

What can I expect from a session?

We work on a concrete problem that matters to you: pairing in your repository, reviewing a pull request, or comparing architecture options. I ask questions and explain trade-offs rather than just fixing the code for you. You leave with concrete next steps and an understanding you can apply to the next problem.

How often do we meet, and will it fit my schedule?

We typically meet every two or three weeks, with time between sessions to apply what you learn. I’m based in Germany and usually work during European business hours, but we can discuss other time zones on the intro call. If you need to reschedule a session, just let me know.

How much does mentoring cost?

The intro call is free. We’ll discuss your goals and agree on the scope, session schedule, and pricing before you commit to paid mentoring. You don’t need to choose or buy a package before we talk.

Can my employer pay for mentoring?

Yes. I provide business invoices and can describe the learning goals for your manager. If you have a professional development budget, mention it on the intro call.

What if mentoring isn't a good fit?

The intro call gives us both a chance to find out whether working together makes sense, with no obligation to continue. If after your first paid session you don’t feel it’s a good fit, I’ll refund you.

How do I get started?

Fill out the short inquiry form above with your background, goals, and availability. I’ll review your answers and reply by email. If I think I can help, I’ll invite you to a free 30-minute intro call to discuss fit, expectations, and next steps. There’s no obligation to continue.
