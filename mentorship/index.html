Frequently Asked Questions

Is this for me?

This mentorship is designed for experienced engineers who already know how to build software and now have a real Rust project, codebase, or review responsibility. It is especially useful when your experience comes from Python, Go, Java, C++, or another language whose familiar patterns do not translate directly to Rust. If you need organization-wide adoption support, team consulting and training are a better fit.

What happens on the free call?

We’ll talk about your background, your current Rust work, and the decisions or skills you want to improve. I’ll explain how I work and tell you honestly whether mentorship, a single session, a free resource, or a different kind of help makes the most sense. There is no obligation and no sales pressure.

Do I need an existing Rust project?

A real project or codebase is ideal because it gives our work context, but it is not mandatory. We can choose a realistic project or use a design and review exercise based on the kind of Rust work you expect to do.

Can I use AI during mentorship?

Yes. AI can generate Rust quickly, but the difficult part is deciding whether the types, boundaries, dependencies, async behavior, and failure modes belong in a production system. We’ll use tools where they help while strengthening the mental models you need to direct and review them. The goal is for you to remain responsible for the design.

What outcomes should I expect?

You should leave sessions with clearer mental models, concrete next steps, and a stronger process for making and reviewing Rust decisions. The goal is not dependence on a mentor; it is becoming able to explain the architecture, review the diff, and handle the next implementation with less help.

What does a session look like?

You set the agenda with my help. We might pair in your repository, review a pull request, compare architecture options, read library or standard-library source, or draw a design. I ask questions and explain trade-offs so that you leave understanding the decision rather than merely copying a fix.

Which option should I start with?

Choose a single session for one problem or to try the working style. Five sessions are the recommended path when you have a concrete project goal and want repeated implementation and review cycles. Ten sessions make sense for a larger system or a longer transition into serious Rust work.

What if I can't make a scheduled session?

Life happens. Just let me know and we’ll move your session to another slot. The 5-session block is valid for 3 months, and the 10-session block for 6 months, so there’s plenty of flexibility.

How do I know you're the right mentor?

That’s what the intro call is for. We’ll talk, you’ll get a feel for my style, and we’ll both know if it’s a good fit. You can also watch this video beforehand to see if you like the way I work. And if after the first paid session it doesn’t feel right, I’ll refund you.

What timezone do you work in?

I’m based in Düsseldorf, Germany (CET/UTC+1). I prefer to schedule sessions during European business hours, but I’m flexible to accommodate other timezones when possible. Some people prefer code reviews via Loom videos, which allows for more asynchronous communication. Please reach out to clarify the details.

Do you offer refunds?

My goal is your success, not trapping you in a contract. If after your first session you don’t feel it’s a good fit, I’ll refund you.

How do I schedule the five sessions?

After purchasing the package, you’ll receive a personal booking link by email. Use the same link whenever you’re ready to schedule your next session. You can choose each date separately and use all five sessions at your own pace within three months.

How does payment work?

You pay upfront for your session package via Stripe. After payment, I’ll email you a personal link for booking the included sessions individually. You’ll receive a receipt from Stripe; contact me if you also need an invoice for professional development expenses. Unused sessions are refunded if things don’t work out.
